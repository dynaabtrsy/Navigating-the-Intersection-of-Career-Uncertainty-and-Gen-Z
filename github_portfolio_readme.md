# Navigating the Intersection of Career Uncertainty & Gen-Z Wellbeing (2020–2024)

> **Redefining the graduate journey: From surviving uncertainty to thriving in stability.**

---

## 📌 1. Overview

Despite record educational attainment and high Labor Force Participation Rates in Malaysia, a significant structural discrepancy persists between the output of tertiary graduates and the creation of high-skilled jobs. This project analyzes the **utilization gap** among Malaysian Generation-Z graduates between 2020 and 2024. 

By integrating multi-domain datasets across labor economics, higher education, and public mental health, this study provides empirical proof that **Skill-Related Underemployment (SRU)**—rather than open unemployment—is the primary driver of psychological distress and career precarity among Gen-Z graduates.

---

## 💡 2. Executive Summary

* **The Illusion of Success:** Malaysia reached **5.98 million graduates in 2024** with an **86% Labor Force Participation Rate** and low open unemployment (~3.76%). However, low unemployment masks a deeper structural flaw.
* **The Structural Reality:** Approximately **35.5% (~1.95 million) to 40.38%** of graduates are trapped in semi-skilled or low-skilled roles for which they are overqualified (*Skill-Related Underemployment*).
* **The Empirical Link:** Using linear regression between SRU rates and DASS-21 mental health indicators, the research proves a direct positive relationship: **Graduate underutilization directly predicts elevated Youth Anxiety, Stress, and Depression scores.**
* **Statistical Convergence:** In **2023**, SRU hit a critical peak of **~42%**, coinciding precisely with the peak in the **Youth Anxiety Index (Score: 34)**.
* **Core Takeaway:** Employment status alone is an insufficient metric for economic health; policy must shift focus toward **high-value role generation** and **mental health as a primary economic indicator**.

### Statistical Formulation
- **Linear Regression Equation:**
  $$\text{Youth Anxiety Index } (Y) = 0.6 \times \text{SRU Rate } (X) + 5$$
- **Primary Metrics Analyzed:**
  - Skill-Related Underemployment (SRU) Rate (%)
  - DASS-21 Distress Metrics (Depression, Anxiety, Stress)
  - Graduate Proliferation Index (GPI) (National baseline threshold = 1.00)

## 🔑 4. Key Findings

### A. The "Vertical Mismatch" & Income Gap
- **Wage Stagnation:** Expected graduate starting salaries average **RM 4,521** (Degree) and **RM 3,390** (Diploma). However, actual median starting wages stagnate around **RM 2,000 – RM 2,500**, creating a severe economic baseline decoupling.
- **Middle-Tier Saturation:** The growth rate of high-skilled vacancies lags far behind graduate supply. The semi-skilled tier acts as an economic "catch-all," absorbing overqualified talent into "survival employment."

### B. High-Risk Demographic Hotspots

| Category | High-Risk Segment | Critical Insight |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ethnicity** | Minority Groups (Bajau, Iban, Bidayuh, Orang Asli) | High unemployment rate, affecting nearly half of the group's graduate population |
| **Field of Study** | Agriculture & Veterinary | Highest average unemployment rate among fields of study |
| **Graduate Proliferation Index (GPI)** | STEM Disciplines | GPI is below 1.00 (national average), signaling supply-demand misalignment |
| **Qualification Level** | Diploma Holders | Account for **63.2%** of all unemployed graduates |
| **Skill Tier** | Low-Skilled | Low demand paired with low wage growth |

### C. Psychosocial Impact & Stress Drivers
- **Top Drivers of Stress Variance among Gen-Z Graduates:**
  1. **Job Insecurity:** $51.79\%$ (Leading cause of future-anxiety)
  2. **Work-Pace Conflict:** $36.16\%$ (High-intensity semi-skilled roles without intellectual fulfillment)
  3. **Work-Life Conflict:** $33.88\%$
  4. **Role Dissatisfaction:** $30.36\%$
- **Clinical Prevalence:** Over **60%** of Gen-Z graduates report suffering from high anxiety symptoms, **38%** report depression symptoms, and **10%** exhibit suicidal ideation associated with career uncertainty.

## 🎯 5. Recommendations

1. **Industry-Curriculum Skill Alignment:**
   * *Action:* Continuously align university curricula with real-time market needs using dynamic employer feedback loops.
   * *Impact:* Ensures graduates possess high-demand industry skills from Day 1.
2. **Policy & Tax Infrastructure:**
   * *Action:* Implement tiered tax incentives for enterprises that convert intern placements into permanent, full-time skilled roles.
   * *Impact:* Transitions temporary employment into stable long-term career pathways.
3. **Targeted High-Skilled Role Generation:**
   * *Action:* Expand high-value industries to absorb the ~35%+ of overqualified graduates trapped in low/semi-skilled jobs.
   * *Impact:* Reduces national "brain waste" and boosts labor productivity.
4. **Mental Health as an Economic Metric:**
   * *Action:* Formally integrate Youth Anxiety and Psychosocial Well-being indices into national economic performance scorecards.
   * *Impact:* Acknowledges mental well-being as an integral component of national development.

---

## ⚠️ 6. Limitations

* **Macro-Data Aggregation:** The use of national aggregate statistics (DOSM/MOHE) may smooth out localized state-level or micro-industry variances.
* **Temporal Lag:** Official tracer studies and national health surveys operate on periodic publication schedules, introducing minor time lags in trend correlation.
* **Self-Reported Distress Metrics:** Psychological indices rely on DASS-21 and self-reported survey evaluations, which can be influenced by external socio-economic events (e.g., post-pandemic readjustments).

* **Evidence-Based Advocacy:** Shifts the public and policy narrative from blaming graduate performance to addressing structural market demand and job-tier mismatch.
